## Analytical Footnotes (Methodology & Physics of Metrics)

*The following notes explain mathematical properties of the simulation that may appear counter-intuitive at first glance.*

#### 1. Travel Time Ratio (Actual vs. Ideal) is ~3.0x. Is the ride-sharing algorithm producing massive detours?
* **No.** The average detour per vehicle is only **~11 meters**. The 3x multiplier is caused by the difference between mathematical theory and physical simulation.
* `T_ideal` is calculated purely mathematically, assuming a vehicle drives the shortest path at a constant **30 km/h** without ever stopping.
* `Actual Travel Time` is generated by the physical SUMO simulator. The vehicle physically stops at red lights and intersections down the Manhattan avenues. A 17-minute ride in Manhattan is perfectly realistic, and the 3x delay is entirely composed of physical traffic-light latency, not ride-sharing routing errors.

#### 2. Why is the Predicted `Avg In-Vehicle Time` shorter than the Shortest Path `T_ideal`?
* `T_ideal` was generated using a strict **30 km/h** baseline (8.33 m/s) in the preprocessing pipeline.
* The predictive assignment engine (`file2.py`) originally queried SUMO for estimated travel times, which used the physical speed limits of the Manhattan road network (up to **50 km/h** / 13.89 m/s). Because 50 > 30, the dispatcher mathematically predicted faster times. *(Note: As of the latest patch, this mathematical discrepancy has been unified to strictly use 8.33 m/s for absolute consistency).* 

#### 3. Why is the Fleet Occupancy Rate so low (~0.29 pax/vehicle)?
* **Supply massively outstrips Demand:** 7,743 rides taking ~17 minutes each equals roughly **2,200 hours** of passenger demand. The simulation deploys 3,000 vehicles operating for 2.5 hours, providing **7,500 hours** of fleet supply.
* The fleet is over-provisioned by a factor of 3.x for this specific 1-hour trip slice, resulting in the high Deadheading Ratio (86.6%). The vehicles are empty simply because there are not enough passengers to fill them.

#### 4. Why is the Avg Detour per Vehicle so incredibly low (~11.2 m)?
* The mathematical cost function heavily penalizes detours (`gamma`).
* When a vehicle is completely empty (which is 86.6% of the time), picking up its *first* passenger is considered "Deadheading", and generates a detour penalty (`delta_new`) of **0.0 m**.
* The dispatcher is so strictly constrained that it only assigns secondary passengers if they are standing almost exactly on the primary passenger's existing shortest path. The algorithm outright refuses to loop around city blocks to pack the car.

#### 5. Are any distances reported as Euclidean (Straight-line)?
* **No.** Every single distance on this report is the true, physical road-network distance.
* `Avg Walking Distance` is calculated using a Dijkstra flood-fill directly on the SUMO pedestrian crosswalk/sidewalk graph.
* Euclidean distances are only used internally by the solver as an admissible heuristic to mathematically prune impossible branches to save CPU time. They are never reported as final values.
